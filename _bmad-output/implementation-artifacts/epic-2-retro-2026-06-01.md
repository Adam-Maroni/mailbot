## 2. What Went Well

- **Gate-coverage-only review held as the default cadence and worked.** Epic 1's retro action item #1 formalized "per-story Sonnet review only for boundary-introducing / dep-introducing / dev-self-flagged stories." Epic 2 executed exactly that policy: 2-1 (introduces `INSERT INTO router_calls` boundary + `RouterResult` shapes), 2-2 (introduces `yaml.safe_load` boundary + hot-reload race surface), 2-4 (orchestration capstone) got Sonnet review; 2-3 / 2-5 / 2-6 / 2-7 / 2-8 / 2-9 / 2-10 ran gate-coverage-only. Every gate-only story shipped green-on-first-run. The gates **are** the review surface for mechanical stories.
- **Failure-as-data contract held across 10 stories.** No exception ever crosses the verb boundary. Every adapter error, schema-validation fail, timeout, rate-limit, budget-trip, loop-detect, and pause is a `RouterResult(ok=False, error=RouterError(code=<stable enum>, …))`. The `ErrorCode` enum reached 15 stable members by epic end with zero churn after Story 2-1 shipped it — proving Story 2-1's investment in enumerating-all-values-up-front paid off (see Story 2-1 Dev Notes "Why we enumerate all `model_chosen_reason` values now").
- **Migration renumbering disaster was caught at the right gate and absorbed cleanly.** Epic 1's Story 1-10 added `005_emails_removed_reason.sql` post-retro. Epic 2 spec hard-coded migrations 005–009. Story 2-1 caught this at dev time (the migrations runner's duplicate-prefix detector from Story 1-3 would have crashed startup loudly), renumbered to `006`, propagated the +1 shift through 2-7/2-8/2-9 deterministically, and documented the cumulative chain in each story's Dev Notes. Zero broken migrations shipped.
- **Architectural seams paid off late.** Story 2-4 split the Router into `router.py` + `registry.py` + `escalation.py` + `pricing.py` deliberately so that 2-5 (rate-limit gate), 2-6 (Anthropic adapter via registry), 2-7 (cache wrap + cache-warmer rate-limit bypass), 2-8 (pre-dispatch budget gate + post-dispatch update), and 2-9 (kill-switch flag + loop detector) could bolt onto known seams. Result: no `router.py` ballooning, every bolt-on shipped without refactoring the orchestrator.
- **Boundary checker positive-pass coverage matured.** Story 2-1 review found that AC-8 "allowlist passes clean" had a coverage gap (only the violation case was tested). Both 2-1 and 2-2 now ship parallel `*_in_allowlisted_*_path_passes` tests, plus f-string-bypass-attempt coverage. The boundary checker has gone from "trust the literal" to "trust the AST walk" + "trust the f-string fragments" — same pattern reused in 2-2's `yaml.safe_load` boundary.
- **Audit-writer monopoly enforced cleanly via boundary check, not ruff.** Story 2-1 Dev Notes "Why boundary checker, not ruff" called the right shot: the AC said "ruff blocks" but the right tool for substring-in-allowlist is the AST walker. Reused for `yaml.safe_load` in 2-2. The epic AC phrasing was honored in intent (the gate that runs at `make lint` time fails on violations), and the implementation choice was documented.
- **Test-count growth was linear and consistent across stories.** Each story added on the order of +8 to +24 net new tests (2-1 +47/+24 review, 2-2 +18/+3 review, 2-3 +9, 2-4 +34, 2-5 +22, 2-6 +8, 2-7 +16, 2-8 +15, 2-9 +18, 2-10 +9). No story had a "too few tests, gate-only is not enough" gap.

## 3. What Was Challenging

- **Phase 3.5 cadence (Epic 1 retro action item #2) was NOT executed during the autonomous run.** Epic 1's retro committed to 4 checkpoints across Epic 2 — after 2-1, 2-3, 2-6, and at epic-end. Story 2-6's Anthropic cache-hit behavior is verified via `httpx.MockTransport` recorded fixtures, not against the real provider.
- **Mid-impl boundary widening between stories happened twice — both were caught, but the gate did not catch them.** (1) Story 2-1's `_ESCALATED_FROM_RE` regex was tightened to `r"^escalated_from_[\w.\-]+$"`. Story 2-4 discovered model ids contain colons (`qwen2.5:3b-instruct-q4_K_M`) and widened to `r"^escalated_from_[\w.:\-]+$"`. Same pattern showed up between stories 1-1's epic-backlog gap (W-1) and Story 2-2's ownership of `sensitivity_patterns.yaml` — except Epic 1 retro caught W-1 and made 2-2 own it explicitly.
- **Story 2-1 sub-second audit `ts` precision deferred — risk emerging in 2-5 territory.** Story 2-1's `_utc_z_now()` strftime-pinned at second precision (`len(row.ts) == 20`). Story 2-4 noted: "will likely surface as a real correctness issue when Story 2-5's lane scheduler produces audit rows in tight bursts." Story 2-5 shipped without addressing it. The risk is now in 2-7's cache-warmer interval-task burst territory + 2-9's loop-detector hot-windows where same-second tie-ordering by `id` is reverse of dispatch sequence on escalation. Story 2-9 shipped LoopDetector + AnomalyDetector + PauseState + verbs but **did not refactor dispatch to be queue-based**. `/pause` works at the `ask_router` flag level (calls are blocked at entry), not at a true queue-drain level. Functionally adequate for the kill-switch use case; structurally the two-queue contract is still partially implemented. The AC-4 priority-strict assertion (chat call started after batch finishes before batch) was verified at the inline-dispatch level, not at queue-drain level.
- **Hermes-aux drift fire-once alarm (Story 2-10 AC-9) deferred to Story 6-1.** Story 2-10 ships the SQL aggregator + the breakdown verb, but the actual `notifications.send_informational("Hermes aux drift: 0 calls in last 24h")` fire-once with state persistence (so it fires once per stale episode, not every hourly tick) was kicked into Story 6-1's `mailbot status` CLI. **No structured tracking on `epic-run-flags.md` for this deferral** — captured only in story 2-10's Completion Notes. - **`epic-run-flags.md` was not refreshed at Epic 2 close.** Epic 1's flags file (which I reviewed) is dated 2026-06-01 and is Epic-1-shaped. No Epic-2-equivalent flags file exists. The deferred items (sub-second `ts`, unpriced-model 0.0 cost, Hermes drift alarm, ruff rule for `caller_origin`, queue-based dispatch) are scattered across story Completion Notes rather than aggregated. ## 4. Key Insights

1. **Once a boundary lands, every subsequent story should reuse the boundary-test pattern.** Story 2-1 found the positive-pass-coverage gap mid-review (R8) and fixed it. Story 2-2 reused the same pattern: violation fixture + allowlisted-path passes + f-string bypass attempt. This is the canonical shape of a boundary test going forward — codify it.
2. **Gate-coverage-only worked for mechanical stories built on top of well-specified seams.** Stories 2-3, 2-5, 2-6, 2-7, 2-8, 2-9, 2-10 had clear ACs, tight integration-test surfaces against real SQLite + `httpx.MockTransport`, and bolted onto seams that 2-4 left for them. Gate-coverage-only was sufficient. The pattern: when the boundary + the orchestrator are stable, mechanical stories don't need adversarial review.
3. **Capstone stories (2-4) and boundary-introducing stories (2-1, 2-2) still merit per-story Sonnet review.** 2-4 found a HIGH escalation-cap bug that gate-coverage would have shipped (the test comment was factually wrong — review caught both the bug and the comment). 2-1 found a HIGH sanitize_error dead-code bug. 2-2 found a HIGH `SKIP_DB`-vs-policy decoupling bug. None of these are surfaceable by gate-coverage alone — they need a second model walking the code.
4. **Deferred items need a flags file, not just story Completion Notes.** Epic 1's `epic-run-flags.md` aggregation made deferred items first-class. Epic 2's lack of the same artifact scatters the deferrals. Action: re-establish the flags-file discipline at every epic close, not just when the autonomous-epic-run skill emits one.
5. **Phase 3.5 cadence requires a human checkpoint that the autonomous run cannot self-execute.** The autonomous-epic-run skill ships green code but cannot verify the integrated Docker stack against real external surfaces (Ollama warmup + Anthropic real call). Epic 1's retro action item #2 committed to 4 checkpoints; Epic 2's run skipped all of them. The action item was correct; the execution gap is the user's, not the agent's. Forward: Phase 3.5 needs to be a `/loop` or explicit `--phase-3.5-checkpoint` step the user runs before the next epic kicks off.
6. **Cross-story Literal/regex tightening is a recurring smell.** The pattern of "Story N defines a closed set X; Story N+k needs X+{new_value}; widening is silent unless someone reads ahead" appeared twice in this epic (`_ESCALATED_FROM_RE`, `model_chosen_reason`). Forward: when a story ships a regex/Literal that downstream stories will populate, mark it explicitly in the story's Dev Notes (Story 2-1 did this for `model_chosen_reason`; it did not for `_ESCALATED_FROM_RE`). Make this a checklist item: "Are there downstream Literal/regex extensions this story should anticipate?"
7. **Audit-write-failure-masks-original-error is a discipline that paid off.** Story 2-1 review finding R7 (MEDIUM) caught that `record_router_call`'s exception would mask the real Router error in 2-4's `finally` block. The fix (`try/except` inside `record_router_call` that logs-and-swallows) was applied in 2-1, and Story 2-4 shipped without re-discovering the issue. Right call, right time.

## 13. Postscript (added 2026-06-01) — W-5 resolved: Option B (bytes + companion columns)

After the main retrospective concluded, Adam and Amelia held a focused discussion on the W-5 blocker (embedding BLOB dtype/shape contract). The three options on the table were:

- **(A)** JSON array — `json.dumps(vec.tolist())`. Self-describing, debuggable, but ~4× storage overhead.
- **(B)** Raw bytes + companion `embedding_dtype` + `embedding_shape` columns. Tight storage, fast read path, schema-level self-documentation. Requires write-discipline mitigated by the writer-monopoly pattern (same shape as Story 2-1's `record_router_call`).
- **(C)** numpy `.npy` format inline with self-describing header. Officially cross-numpy-version stable; ~128 byte header overhead per row; depends on numpy at every read site.

**Decision: Option B.** Rationale:

1. **Best storage-and-read profile.** Raw little-endian float32 bytes = 3072 bytes per row for `nomic-embed-text`'s 768-dim output. Reading is a direct `np.frombuffer(...)` — no parse, no header skip.
2. **Write-discipline risk has a known mitigation in the codebase.** Story 2-1 established the writer-monopoly pattern (one helper function + boundary check on the SQL literal + AST walker covering f-strings). Story 3-4 reuses this verbatim for `write_embedding()`.
3. **Companion columns make the contract visible in the schema** rather than buried in code. A future maintainer reading the migration sees `embedding_dtype` next to `embedding` and immediately knows the format.
4. **The choice is reversible.** If a year from now Option C looks better, a one-shot migration re-encodes all rows. The embeddings are recomputable from email bodies anyway. This is a serialization choice, not an architectural invariant.

**The non-obvious detail:** the dtype string must be **little-endian explicit** (`'<f4'` for float32). The writer does `vector.astype('<f4').tobytes()`; the reader does `numpy.frombuffer(blob, dtype=row.embedding_dtype)` — never `dtype='f4'` or `dtype=numpy.float32` (both default to native byte order, which silently corrupts on cross-architecture deploy). Captured in the migration as a CHECK constraint on `embedding_dtype` (must start with `<`) — *no, on reflection, captured as a unit test on the writer instead, since CHECK constraints would block a future migration to a different dtype*. The test asserts `vec.astype('<f4').tobytes() == row.embedding` byte-for-byte against a fixed-content vector.

**Downstream changes shipped as part of this resolution:**

1. **Story 3-1 epic spec amended** (`_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/epics.md` line ~1088): migration renumbered `010_derived_fields.sql` → `011_derived_fields.sql` (per Epic 2's cumulative +1 chain). The `embedding` column gains two NEW companion columns — `embedding_dtype` (TEXT, nullable) and `embedding_shape` (TEXT, nullable, JSON-encoded shape tuple). The W-5 resolution is referenced inline in the AC body.
2. **Story 3-4 epic spec amended** (line ~1207): introduces `write_embedding()` as the sole writer of `emails.embedding` + companion columns. Boundary check on `emails.embedding` writes added with positive-pass + f-string-bypass coverage (reusing Story 2-1's pattern). Cross-architecture portability test added — asserts `vec.astype('<f4').tobytes()` round-trips byte-for-byte. Reader uses companion columns (`numpy.frombuffer(blob, dtype=row.embedding_dtype).reshape(json.loads(row.embedding_shape))`); no hard-coded dtype/shape assumptions.
3. **Architecture doc patch owed** — Winston to add a paragraph under `architecture.md` § AR-SCHEMA-2 documenting the contract: little-endian float32 raw bytes, two companion columns, writer-monopoly enforcement, reader pattern. Same shape as the AR-D11-2 mid-call race semantics paragraph. (This was Epic 1 retro action item #5; closing it out as part of W-5 resolution.)

**Critical-path update:**

- **W-5 is RESOLVED.** Epic 3 Story 3-1 is now strictly unblocked. The other process items from the retro action list (Phase 3.5 cadence, epic-run-flags catch-up, Literal/regex checklist) remain as quality improvements but are not hard blockers.
- **Migration-numbering policy in architecture.md** (Epic 1 retro action item #3, Epic 2 retro action item #10) — still owed but the Epic 3 spec now documents the cumulative +1 shift inline, so this is documentation hygiene rather than a structural blocker.
- The decision was made in conversation rather than via formal architectural review. Recorded here so the rationale is preserved for future maintainers. If Winston disagrees with the choice, this is the artifact to push back against — the implementation has not yet shipped (Story 3-1 is `backlog`).

**Process lesson:**

W-5 felt like a Big Architectural Question for three reasons that turned out to be illusory: three "valid" options with no clearly-wrong answer triggered analysis paralysis; the "first write commits the format forever" framing felt load-bearing but was reversible; the domain (dtypes, endianness, serialization formats) was unfamiliar relative to Epics 1-2's OAuth + SQLite + FastAPI territory. The actual decision had bounded consequences (storage cost, read latency, cross-arch portability — all measurable), well-known mitigations (writer-monopoly pattern from Story 2-1), and was reversible via a one-shot migration. **Action item for future retros:** when a blocker feels architectural, distinguish "irreversible cross-cutting invariant" (genuinely hard — sensitivity-first, Rule Ω, idempotency keys) from "local serialization choice" (felt hard, isn't — embedding format, log line format, JSON shape). The former merits multi-stakeholder review; the latter should be a 30-minute decision documented inline.
