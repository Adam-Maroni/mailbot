## 2. What Went Well

- **Phase 3.5 cadence FINALLY executed.** Three consecutive retros (Epic 1, 2, 3) carried "execute Phase 3.5 catch-up" as an unfulfilled commitment. Epic 4 ships an agent-run Phase 3.5 walkthrough with 11/11 reachable checkpoints PASS — CP-1 type-foundation, CP-3 migrations, CP-4..CP-5 propose_action happy/refusal paths, CP-6..CP-7 mint_grant + defender-bias refusals, CP-8 cooling-off + cancel race-safe no-op, CP-9..CP-10 sensitivity token mint/consume/single-use/TTL/wrong-task + confidential/normal refusals, CP-11 revert end-to-end. Performed against a fresh test DB without the Docker stack (drainer + adapter aren't wired into `worker.py` yet — Epic 6's job). **Final DB inventory recorded with row-level evidence.** Zero findings discovered during the walk; the strong gate-coverage caught what would have surfaced. The "structural skill amendment" from Epic 3 retro action #3 wasn't shipped — but the walk got executed anyway. The lesson: even a synthesized checklist walkthrough has value; do it always.
- **Sub-second `ts` precision overdue across three epics — STILL not addressed.** (Moved to §3 — this is a failure carryover, not a success.)
- **Verb-API tier-enforcement is structural, not behavioral.** Story 4-2's `propose_action` rejects any payload containing a `tier` field with `TIER_PROMOTION_ATTEMPT` BEFORE the tier is computed. Story 4-1's boundary check makes any bare-string `"delete"` / `"mark_read"` etc. outside `types.py` a lint failure. FR-5.6 "agent cannot promote tier" is now enforceable by construction — not by trust in the agent. **The defense-in-depth shape used for FR-2.5 (Epic 3) reuses cleanly:** boundary at the lint layer + tier-promotion guard at the verb layer + 18-value CHECK constraint in migration 015 at the SQL layer. Three layers, each independently sufficient.
- **Privacy invariant for sensitive-API handshake follows Epic 3 §4 insight #3 (defense-in-depth).** Story 4-7 ships mint-time validation (`mint_sensitivity_token` refuses confidential + refuses normal + only mints for sensitive) AND consume-time validation (`ask_router(confirmation_token=...)` re-validates against in-memory registry + email-id binding + task-type binding + single-use + 10-min TTL). Two layers — exactly what Story 3-3 codified for FR-2.5 (startup + per-call). The pattern travels.
- **In-memory token registry honors AR-D12-1 by design.** Tokens die on worker process restart by design — "forcing re-confirmation." A SQLite-backed registry would survive across restarts (undesirable). The audit trail still survives via `router_calls.sensitivity_grant_id`, so consume events are forensically recoverable even after the registry is gone. The 16-hex-char `grant_id` (sha256 of the 43-char `secrets.token_urlsafe(32)`) is short enough for log lines and long enough for collision-free identity at plausible mint rates. **The architectural decision shipped without re-litigation across mint, consume, audit, and tests.**
- **Email-less Tier-3 mismatch resolved cross-story (4-2 CR-2 → 4-4 implementation).** Story 4-1's `ACTION_PROPERTIES` says every Tier-3 action has `change_marker_required=True`. Story 4-2 documented that email-less Tier-3 rows (MODIFY_INBOX_RULE/FILTER, TOUCH_DELEGATED_MAILBOX, SEND_NEW_EMAIL) store `change_marker_at_propose=NULL` because there's no source email. Story 4-4's drainer resolves cleanly: skip the strict-ETag check when `email_id IS NULL` while still going through grant-validation. The `change_marker_required=True` property still informs Story 4-5's per-call retry policy. **Cross-story coordination via the deferred-items aggregation worked as intended — the gap surfaced in 4-2 CR, was tracked in epic-run-flags, and was closed in 4-4.**
- **MODIFY_INBOX_RULE vs MODIFY_OUTLOOK_FILTER cross-story decision (4-1 CR-1) resolved in 4-5.** Both action types dispatch to the same Graph endpoint (`/me/mailFolders/inbox/messageRules`); the payload distinguishes via optional `payload["rule_kind"]`. The two enum members preserved for audit-trail granularity (a "filter" and a "rule" are semantically distinct to Adam, even if they hit the same Graph API). Same shape as the 4-2 → 4-4 closure: deferred-to-downstream-story works when the downstream story actually closes the loop.
- **AR-D5-1 retry chain shipped error-classified.** Story 4-5's `OutlookGraphWriteAdapter` ships 429/503/timeout retry, 4xx fail-fast, 5xx-non-503 retry-once. The classification is per-error-shape, not per-numeric-code. Tested with `httpx.MockTransport` covering every branch (10 tests). The "fail-fast on 4xx" branch is the load-bearing correctness property — don't retry a permanent failure. **Mocked at the network boundary only** (per the Middleware-Real-Bootstrap MailBot reframing); the dispatch table + retry classifier + budget-on-failure are exercised by real code paths.
- **Hard 20-send/day cap (AR-D5-2) enforces failure-counts-too.** Story 4-6 ships `DAILY_SEND_CAP=20` enforcement in the drainer with `budget_consumed=true` flipped on BOTH success AND terminal failure. Subtle correctness invariant, shipped tested.

## 4. Key Insights

1. **Aspirational action items do not bind a context-pressured orchestrator.** Epic 3 retro action #1 (CR cadence v2 codified in skill BEFORE 4.7) and Phase 3.5 commitment (Epic 1 → 2 → 3 retros) are the canonical examples: well-reasoned text in a retro doc, no structural binding in the skill, the orchestrator under context pressure makes the operational call. The text is necessary but insufficient. **Either the skill changes, or the same outcome ships next epic.** The Phase 3.5 walkthrough that DID ship in Epic 4 was triggered by Adam-side credential capture (Story 4-0) being structurally required for the autonomous run to validate against real services — i.e., a *structural* constraint, not an aspirational commitment. **Patterns that ship: structural constraints. Patterns that don't ship: aspirational commitments.**
2. **Defense-in-depth is the right shape for both privacy (Epic 3) and authorization (Epic 4) invariants.** Epic 3 §4 insight #3 codified defense-in-depth for FR-2.5 (startup + per-call). Epic 4 ships the same shape for FR-5.6 (lint boundary + verb guard + SQL CHECK) and for AR-D12-1 sensitivity handshake (mint-time + consume-time). **Three independent layers, each independently sufficient, no single point of failure.** This is now the canonical shape for any invariant the system MUST enforce. Codify in `architecture.md` as Rule H (or similar) — "every invariant is defended at the lint/boundary layer + the verb layer + the data layer."
3. **The "store-just-written-value-in-memory" reflex (Epic 3 insight #7) scales to cross-story coordination.** Story 4-1 + 4-2 ship `ActionType` + `EMAIL_LESS_ACTIONS` registry. Story 4-4's drainer reads from the registry rather than re-deriving tier from a string. Story 4-5's adapter reads from the registry rather than maintaining its own dispatch logic. **One source of truth, consumed by every downstream story.** Same shape as carrying `class_coarse` in memory rather than re-querying — but at the cross-story scale. The registry pattern (frozen Pydantic model + `MappingProxyType`-wrapped dict + helper trio) is now reusable for any cross-cutting properties table.
4. **`MappingProxyType` is the canonical way to ship a read-only dict in Python.** Story 4-1 wraps `ACTION_PROPERTIES` in `MappingProxyType` so downstream stories can't mutate it. `del _PROPS` deliberately blocks the "factory-then-discard" pattern. Pydantic `frozen=True` on the model itself. **Three layers of immutability** for a constant that's load-bearing across 8 stories + the lint boundary. The CR-3 finding (`del _PROPS`) was the clarifying move that made the read-only contract structural.
5. **Atomic-claim-via-UPDATE-with-rowcount-check is a portable race-safe primitive.** Story 1-7 sync delta_link claim. Story 4-4 drainer pending_actions claim. Story 4-6 cancel_action cooling_off claim. Three independent uses across three epics, same pattern. **Codify in `architecture.md` as Rule I** (or AR-PAT-N) — "claim-by-conditional-UPDATE with rowcount-1 confirmation; race-safe by construction." The pattern is mature enough to be a one-paragraph recipe.
6. **Mocked at the network boundary only is the Middleware-Real-Bootstrap reframing.** Story 4-5's adapter tests use `httpx.MockTransport` at the network boundary while exercising the real retry classifier + real budget logic + real dispatch table. Story 4-4's drainer tests use `FakeGraphWriteAdapter` while exercising the real claim-and-drain logic. **The mocking surface is narrow and ABOVE the boundary; everything below is real.** This is the MailBot reframing of the "no mocks above the adapter boundary" rule, and it's now applied across 3 epics consistently. Codify as Rule J expansion: "mocks live exactly at the adapter boundary; the dispatch + classification + budget + audit code paths are always real in tests."
7. **The "deferred-to-downstream-story" pattern works when the downstream story actually closes the loop.** Story 4-1 CR-1 (MODIFY_INBOX_RULE vs MODIFY_OUTLOOK_FILTER) → resolved in 4-5. Story 4-2 CR-2 (email-less Tier-3 mismatch) → resolved in 4-4. Both closed cleanly because: (a) the deferred-item entry in `epic-run-flags.md` named the downstream story by number, and (b) the downstream story's Dev Notes explicitly referenced the deferred item. **The "name the closer" + "reference back" handshake is what makes the pattern work.** The Hermes-aux cache double-wrap bug (Story 3-2 CR-8) → "Story 6-N or Epic 5 followup" is the counter-example: no named closer, no reference back, still unresolved across two epics.
8. **A synthesized Phase 3.5 checklist is better than no Phase 3.5.** Epic 4 had no UAT story; checkpoints were synthesized from each story's primary AC. Eleven checkpoints, eleven passes, zero findings. **The walk paid for itself NOT by finding new bugs, but by proving the surface works end-to-end on a fresh DB.** The "we shipped tests + gates + a synthesized walk" tier is more trustworthy than the "we shipped tests + gates" tier — even with the synthesized walk's limitations. Adam-side Phase 3.5 (against real Outlook + real Anthropic via Story 4-0's credential capture) caught 5 latent bugs in 4-0 that gates alone missed. **The lesson is the cadence matters more than the formality.**
